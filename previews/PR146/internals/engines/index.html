GameTracing.FisherRaoChange — Type
FisherRaoChange(ranges)

The largest Fisher–Rao distance any player's mixed strategy moves between two points [w; λ], each player's logits z = λ w in its range of ranges, against its first strategy (fisher_rao_spread).

GameTracing.FisherRaoSettled — Type
FisherRaoSettled(ranges, tolerance, rounding)

PathFollowing's converged under StrategyDistance, each player's logits in its range of ranges. The last correction, times Newton's scale, moved no player's strategy further than tolerance plus rounding times its logits' spread (fisher_rao_spread). That product bounds the Fisher–Rao distance a relative rounding of rounding in each logit moves the strategy.

GameTracing.default_split_check — Method

PathFollowingSettings' default split check: trigger 1.3 and tolerance 10 times distance's settle tolerance. At growth 1.1 a step held by a step-control target rides at f near 1. So trigger 1 fired on up to 67% of logitnashspeed's steps and cost up to 33% per game (median). Trigger 1.3 fired on 0.4-2.7% of them and cost at most 2%. A hunt over 20,000 two-action games and 1200 unfolded pitchforks found the same 7 jumps in Float32 and Float64. Trigger 1.3 fired on and refused each; the smallest jump f was 1.41. At growth 2 with the summed metric, jumps came as low as f 1.1: a looser step control needs trigger 1. A zero settle tolerance gives the tolerance no scale, so it throws an ArgumentError that asks for an explicit `splitcheck`.

GameTracing.fisher_rao_spread — Method

A player's move from from to to, its logits in range, and its logits' spread at to. The move is the Fisher–Rao distance 4 asin(h / 2), h the Euclidean distance between the square roots of the two strategies: a geodesic on the probability simplex, at most π. A small move of logits Δz has length √Var_π(Δz). The spread is the root mean square √(Σ π z²) of the logits z = λ w[range] at to under their own mixed strategy π.

GameTracing.gambit_settings — Method
gambit_settings(E, max_accel)

BifurcationKitSettings in E for pygambit's max_accel, which caps each step's growth (StepControl's max_step_growth, and aggressiveness = max_accel - 1). BifurcationKit's growth limit must exceed 1, so at max_accel = 1 step control only refuses steps. Zero aggressiveness then holds an accepted step's length, where Gambit also shrinks it after a poor corrector. The first step comes from the game (opening_step), as in PathFollowingSettings. The trace steps in default_step_metric, which PathFollowingSettings shares.
